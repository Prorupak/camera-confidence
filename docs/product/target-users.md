# Camera Confidence — Target Users

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the users Camera Confidence is initially designed to help.

The current target-user definition is a product hypothesis based on the problem we are trying to solve.

It should evolve as we observe real users and gather evidence.

---

## 2. Primary User

### People who feel uncomfortable speaking on camera

Our primary user is someone who:

- Wants to become more comfortable speaking on camera.
- Feels nervous, awkward, or self-conscious when being recorded.
- Knows that practice could help.
- Has difficulty practicing consistently on their own.
- Wants a private environment to practice.
- Is willing to complete short speaking exercises.
- Wants supportive guidance rather than harsh evaluation.

The most important characteristic is **camera discomfort**, not age, profession, location, or technical ability.

---

## 3. Primary User Need

The primary user wants to be able to:

> **Press record without feeling overwhelmed, complete a short speaking task, understand what they can improve, and try again.**

Their immediate need is not to become an expert speaker.

Their immediate need is:

> **"I want to feel more comfortable being on camera."**

---

## 4. Primary User Journey

A typical primary user may experience:

```text
I know I should practice
        ↓
I don't know what to practice
        ↓
I feel nervous before recording
        ↓
I record
        ↓
I don't like the result
        ↓
I don't know what to improve
        ↓
I either retry repeatedly or stop
```

Camera Confidence should aim to change this into:

```text
I want to practice
        ↓
I get a small challenge
        ↓
I know what to do
        ↓
I record
        ↓
I receive supportive reflection
        ↓
I know what to improve
        ↓
I try again
        ↓
I gradually become more comfortable
```

# 5. Potential User Segments

The following groups may experience the problem and are potential users.

They are **not yet validated market segments**.

## 5.1 Job Seekers

People preparing for situations such as:

- Job interviews
- Video interviews
- Self-introductions
- Recorded application responses

Potential need:

> Practice speaking clearly and confidently when being recorded.

---

## 5.2 Professionals

People who regularly communicate through cameras.

Potential situations include:

- Video meetings
- Presentations
- Remote communication
- Recorded explanations

Potential need:

> Become more comfortable communicating professionally on camera.

---

## 5.3 Content Creators

People creating video content who feel uncomfortable appearing on camera.

Potential situations include:

- Talking-head videos
- Short-form videos
- Introductions
- Educational content
- Personal videos

Potential need:

> Become comfortable speaking naturally while being recorded.

---

## 5.4 Students

Students who need to communicate through video.

Potential situations include:

- Presentations
- Recorded assignments
- Interviews
- Introductions

Potential need:

> Practice before recording an important presentation or response.

---

# 6. Primary vs. Secondary Users

For the early product, we should avoid designing equally for every potential segment.

### Primary

**People who personally want to become more comfortable speaking on camera.**

### Secondary

- Job seekers
- Professionals
- Content creators
- Students

The secondary segments provide useful contexts for challenges, but the underlying problem remains the same:

> **Discomfort with speaking on camera.**

---

# 7. Who We Are NOT Optimizing For

The initial product is not specifically designed for:

### People who are already highly comfortable on camera

They may use the product for improvement, but they are not the initial focus.

### People looking primarily for video editing

Camera Confidence is not a video editing application.

### People looking for a social video platform

The initial experience is private practice, not publishing or sharing.

### People looking for general AI conversation

Camera Confidence is not intended to be a generic AI chatbot.

### People requiring clinical or therapeutic support

Camera Confidence is a practice tool, not a medical or therapeutic product.

# 8. User Motivation

The user's motivation may come from different situations.

Examples:

```text
"I need to record a video for a job."
```

```text
"I have an interview coming up."
```

```text
"I hate seeing myself on camera."
```

```text
"I want to make videos but I'm uncomfortable appearing in them."
```

```text
"I want to improve my presentation skills."
```

Although the immediate motivations differ, the initial product opportunity is similar:

> **The person wants to become more comfortable communicating through a camera.**

---

# 9. User Constraints

The initial experience should assume that users may:

- Have limited time.
- Feel nervous before recording.
- Not know what to practice.
- Be uncomfortable watching themselves.
- Want to practice privately.
- Prefer short exercises over long sessions.
- Be sensitive to judgment or negative feedback.

These assumptions should influence the product experience, but they should be validated through actual usage.

---

# 10. User Emotional State

The emotional state before recording is particularly important.

A user may feel:

- Nervous
- Self-conscious
- Awkward
- Uncertain
- Frustrated
- Afraid of making mistakes
- Concerned about how they look or sound

Therefore, the product should avoid increasing unnecessary pressure.

The interface should communicate:

> **You are here to practice, not to perform perfectly.**

---

# 11. What the User Wants From the Product

The user should not have to figure out the entire practice process themselves.

They should receive:

### A clear task

> "Introduce yourself in 30 seconds."

### A simple environment

> Camera → Prompt → Record

### Low-pressure expectations

> "Just give it a try."

### Useful reflection

> "Your introduction was clear. Try slowing down slightly next time."

### A reason to continue

> "Ready for another attempt?"

---

# 12. User Success

From the user's perspective, success is not necessarily a high score.

Success might be:

- Pressing record without excessive hesitation.
- Completing the challenge.
- Feeling okay about the recording.
- Understanding one thing to improve.
- Voluntarily trying again.
- Feeling slightly more comfortable than before.
- Returning for another practice session.

The long-term outcome is:

> **The user feels increasingly comfortable communicating on camera.**

---

# 13. User Progression

The product should support a gradual progression.

```text
Beginner
   ↓
Comfortable Starting
   ↓
Comfortable Speaking
   ↓
Comfortable Watching
   ↓
Comfortable Repeating
   ↓
Comfortable With More Difficult Tasks
```

This progression is more important than assigning users a static skill label.

The system should eventually learn from user behavior and adapt challenge difficulty.

---

# 14. Initial User Persona

For product decisions, we can use a simple working persona.

### The Reluctant Recorder

> Someone who knows they need to practice speaking on camera but hesitates every time they have to press record.

They might:

- Write what they want to say beforehand.
- Restart recordings multiple times.
- Watch their recording and focus on mistakes.
- Feel awkward hearing their own voice.
- Speak differently when the camera is on.
- Avoid practicing unless there is an immediate need.

Their desired outcome:

> **"I want recording myself to feel normal."**

This persona is intentionally behavioral rather than demographic.

---

# 15. User Jobs To Be Done

Potential jobs include:

### Functional Job

> Help me practice speaking on camera.

### Emotional Job

> Help me feel less nervous when I am being recorded.

### Progress Job

> Help me see that I am getting better.

### Preparation Job

> Help me prepare for an upcoming camera-based situation.

The initial product should prioritize the **functional + emotional jobs**.

---

# 16. User Segmentation — Future

As we collect real usage data, we may discover that different groups require different experiences.

Potential future segmentation could include:

```text
Camera Confidence
│
├── General Practice
│
├── Interview Practice
│
├── Presentation Practice
│
├── Content Creator Practice
│
└── Professional Communication
```

We should not build these as separate products prematurely.

They should initially be treated as different **practice contexts** built on top of the same core practice loop.

---

# 17. What We Need to Validate

The current target-user definition contains assumptions.

We need to learn:

### Problem intensity

How uncomfortable do users actually feel on camera?

### Frequency

How often do they need to communicate through a camera?

### Existing behavior

How do they currently practice?

### Motivation

Why do they want to become more comfortable?

### Practice willingness

Will they actually record themselves voluntarily?

### Feedback preference

What kind of feedback feels useful rather than judgmental?

### Retention

Will they return and practice again?

### Segment differences

Do job seekers, professionals, creators, and students actually have different needs?

---

# 18. Validation Signals

### Weak signal

> "I can relate to this."

### Better signal

> "I would try this."

### Strong signal

> The user actually records themselves.

### Stronger signal

> The user voluntarily retries.

### Very strong signal

> The user returns for another practice session.

### Strongest early product signal

> **The user repeatedly practices because they believe the product is helping them become more comfortable on camera.**

---

# 19. Current Target-User Hypothesis

Our current hypothesis is:

> **Camera Confidence's primary user is a person who wants to become more comfortable speaking on camera but finds recording themselves uncomfortable, unstructured, or difficult to evaluate.**

This hypothesis should remain open to change.

We should allow real user behavior to determine whether this is actually the right target.

---

# 20. Product Decision Rule

When deciding whether to build a feature, ask:

> **Does this make the experience meaningfully better for someone who is uncomfortable speaking on camera?**

If yes, investigate it.

If it primarily benefits an unrelated user group, it should probably wait.

---

## 21. One-Line Definition

> **Our primary user is someone who wants to become comfortable speaking on camera, but struggles to practice consistently because recording themselves feels uncomfortable and unstructured.**
